    A workspace that works like you do.

    Some tasks need one window. Others need a whole collection. Frames is an open-source window manager for macOS that lets you arrange apps in nested layouts, keep related windows in tabs, and move between the details and the bigger picture.

    Start with a simple split. Add another window. Save a view you like. Your workspace can grow with your ideas.

    Small details. A big difference.

    Your apps, side by side. Or tab by tab.

    Group windows from different apps in one tab stack. Switch between them, reorder tabs, or tear one off when it needs its own space. Your native app windows stay open.

    Go from one thing to everything.

    Frame a single window, move to its neighbour, or zoom out to your workspace. Save up to nine views per display and return to the arrangement you need.

    At home on your Mac.

    Frames runs locally, with no account or subscription. Layouts and preferences stay on your Mac. The project uses native macOS frameworks and is licensed under MIT.

    Get to know your new workspace.

    1. Start with the sample.

      Try the guided workspace without granting permissions or moving your own windows.

    2. Make room on your Mac.

      Enable Accessibility to arrange real app windows. Choose a preset, dock a window at an edge, or build your own nested layout.

    3. Make it yours.

      Adjust gaps, set shortcuts, exclude apps, and save your favourite views. Use layout undo or Float All to return to your original positions.

    Before you get started

    System requirements

    • macOS 14 or later
    • Apple silicon tested; Intel builds untested
    • Source builds: Swift 6 and Xcode Command Line Tools

    Permissions, explained

    Accessibility lets Frames move and resize windows. Screen Recording is optional for window previews; those images stay in memory. You choose which permissions to enable.

    A little help goes a long way.

    Can I try Frames without moving my windows?

    Yes. The sample workspace is separate from your real arrangement and works without permissions. Guided setup and the menu-bar tutorial introduce docking, tabs, focus, and saved views.

    What if I want my old arrangement back?

    Layout undo lets you step back through changes. Float All restores the original visible positions of managed windows, and quitting restores them too. User-minimised windows stay minimised.

    Do I need to enable screen previews?

    No. Without Screen Recording permission, small-window tiles use app icons. Optional still previews are held in memory and are not uploaded.

    Is Frames available to download?

    Frames is currently in development. A public download and repository link will be added here when they are ready.
